Rani Lakshmibai of Jhansi (1828-1858)

Jhansi Ki Rani | Warrior Queen | First Woman Martyr of 1857

Full Name: Manikarnika (Manu) / Lakshmibai Newalkar
Born: 19 Nov 1828, Varanasi, Uttar Pradesh
Parents: Moropant Tambe (Peshwa's advisor) & Bhagirathi Bai
Husband: Maharaja Gangadhar Rao Newalkar, King of Jhansi (Married 1842 at age 14)
Son: Damodar Rao (Born 1851, Adopted - Original Anand Rao)
Education: Horse riding, Sword fighting, Archery, Mallakhamb, Scriptures
Died: 18 June 1858, Gwalior, MP - Died in battle at age 29

1. Early Life - From Manikarnika to Chhabili to Lakshmibai

Manikarnika was born in a Maharashtrian Karhade Brahmin family in Varanasi. Her mother Bhagirathi died when she was 4 years old. Her father Moropant worked for Peshwa Baji Rao II in Bithoor near Kanpur. Peshwa loved her like his daughter and called her Chhabili (playful). In Peshwa's palace, she grew up with Nana Sahib and Tatya Tope - who later became heroes of 1857. She was different from other girls - she learnt horse riding, sword fighting, shooting, mallakhamb from childhood. She could ride horse with both hands holding swords. She was educated in Sanskrit and Marathi scriptures. In 1842, at age 14, she was married to 35-year-old Raja Gangadhar Rao of Jhansi - a wealthy kingdom in Bundelkhand. After marriage, her name became Rani Lakshmibai after Goddess Lakshmi. Jhansi people called her Rani.

2. Queen of Jhansi & Tragedies (1842-1853)

1842-1851 - Happy Life: Jhansi was prosperous - 4000 sq km area. Gangadhar Rao was a good ruler but had no child. Rani organized women's army called Durga Dal and trained women in warfare.

1851 - Birth of Son: In 1851, Rani gave birth to a son Damodar Rao who died after 4 months due to illness. Raja became very depressed and fell sick.

1853 - Adoption and King's Death: To save kingdom from British Doctrine of Lapse, Raja adopted his cousin's 5-year-old son Anand Rao on 20 Nov 1853, one day before his death. He named him Damodar Rao. On 21 Nov 1853, Raja Gangadhar Rao died. British were present during adoption. Rani was just 25-year-old widow. According to Hindu tradition, she broke her bangles and became white-saree widow, but she did not sit quiet - she decided to fight.

3. Doctrine of Lapse & British Injustice (1853-1857)

Lord Dalhousie, Governor-General of India, had made a cruel policy called Doctrine of Lapse - If a king dies without natural heir, his kingdom will be annexed by British East India Company. Adopted son will not be allowed.

Despite written assurance from Gangadhar Rao, British rejected adoption. On 27 Feb 1854, British officer Major Ellis came and told Rani to leave Jhansi fort with Rs 5000 pension. Rani's reply became immortal: "Main apni Jhansi nahi dungi" (I will not give my Jhansi).

British seized Jhansi treasury, jewels worth crores. Rani was forced to leave fort and live in Rani Mahal palace in city. But she secretly prepared army - She collected arms, repaired Jhansi fort walls, made new cannons named Ghanshyaam and Kadak Bijli, and recruited 14,000 soldiers including women soldiers - Jhalkari Bai, Sundar-Mundar, Kashi Bai. Her best friend was Jhalkari Bai who looked exactly like her and was commander of Durga Dal. Rani kept writing petitions to London but British rejected all.

4. 1857 - The Great Revolt & War of Jhansi (May 1857 - April 1858)

May 1857: Revolt started in Meerut on 10 May. On 5 June 1857, 12th Bengal Native Infantry in Jhansi revolted and killed British officers. They gave Jhansi to Rani. On 7 June, Rani took over administration again as regent of adopted son. She ruled Jhansi efficiently for 10 months - reduced taxes, increased army.

British Accusation: British accused Rani of killing British officers (which she denied - she said mutineers did it). They sent army to capture Jhansi.

March 1858 - Siege of Jhansi: British army under Sir Hugh Rose came with 20,000 soldiers and heavy artillery to attack Jhansi which had only 10,000 soldiers. Rose said "Jhansi is the most fortified place in India".
Siege started on 20 March 1858. Jhansi fort fought for 14 days. Rani herself fired cannons day and night wearing soldier uniform. Her army included Tatya Tope who tried to help from outside but was defeated.

4 April 1858 - Fall of Jhansi: British broke fort wall. British soldiers entered city and killed thousands. Rani decided not to surrender. At midnight of 4 April, with adopted son Damodar Rao tied on her back (12 years old), with 300 soldiers including Jhalkari Bai, she jumped from fort wall on her white horse Badal (also called Sarangi). The jump was 30 feet high. Jhalkari Bai disguised as Rani and fought to fool British and was martyred. Rani escaped to Kalpi, 100 km away - rode 24 hours non-stop without water.

British captured Jhansi and looted it. Hugh Rose wrote - "Rani is the bravest and most dangerous of all rebels, she is like Joan of Arc of India".

5. Last Battle - Kalpi to Gwalior to Martyrdom (April - June 1858)

After Jhansi, Rani went to Kalpi and joined Tatya Tope, Rao Sahib (Nana Sahib's nephew). They fought battle of Kalpi on 22 May 1858 but lost due to lack of guns. They then planned to capture Gwalior - Scindia's army was there.

On 1 June 1858, Rani's army entered Gwalior - Scindia Maharaja Jayaji Rao fled to Agra. Rani captured Gwalior fort without much fight - Got treasury and arms. But they wasted 10 days in celebration instead of preparing for British attack - This was biggest mistake.

On 16 June, British under Hugh Rose attacked Gwalior. Final battle was on 17-18 June near Phool Bagh area.
On 18 June 1858, Rani wearing red military uniform with Damodar Rao, fought like tiger. Her horse Badal died near a nullah (canal). New horse refused to jump canal. Two British soldiers attacked - One hit her head with sword, second hit her chest. She was badly wounded but killed both British soldiers. She was carried by loyal soldiers Ramchandra Rao Deshmukh and Narayan Baba to Baba Gangadas Ashram near Gwalior. There she died on 18 June at age 29. She was cremated same day by sadhus before British could find her body. Her last wish was her adopted son should not be harmed - British gave him Rs 200 pension.

British could not find her body - Rose said "She was the best man among rebels".

6. Horse, Sword, Army & Legacy

Weapons: She used two swords (one gifted by her father), pistols, and spear. Her swords weighed 10 kg each - she could fight with both hands.
Horses: Her favourite horses - Sarangi (white), Pavan (brown), Badal (black and white) - All died in wars.
Women Army: She created first women's army in India - Durga Dal led by Jhalkari Bai (who was from Kori caste - Dalit). Jhalkari Bai's bravery is equally famous - She fought disguised as Rani and was killed by British. Sundar, Mundar, Motibai were other commanders.
Memorials: Jhansi Fort has Rani Mahal, Rani's temple, and jump point preserved. Gwalior has her samadhi in Phool Bagh. Statue in Parliament House. Every year 18 June is martyrdom day.
In Poetry: Subhadra Kumari Chauhan's poem "Khoob Ladi Mardaani, Woh Toh Jhansi Wali Rani Thi" is taught to every child.
British Records: Hugh Rose wrote report - "The Rani was remarkable for her bravery, cleverness and perseverance; her generosity to her subordinates was unbounded. She was the most dangerous of all Indian leaders."
She remains the symbol of Indian woman power - At 29, she fought British Empire with son on back, without husband, without kingdom.

"Main apni Jhansi nahi dungi - I shall not surrender my Jhansi"
- Rani Lakshmibai, Feb 1854 to British Officer Ellis
