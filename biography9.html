Indira Gandhi (1917-1984)

First Woman Prime Minister of India | Iron Lady | Bharat Ratna

Full Name: Indira Priyadarshini Gandhi (Nehru)
Born: 19 Nov 1917, Anand Bhavan, Allahabad, UP
Parents: Jawaharlal Nehru (First PM) & Kamala Nehru
Husband: Feroze Gandhi (Journalist & MP) - Married 1942
Children: Rajiv Gandhi (1944-1991) & Sanjay Gandhi (1946-1980)
Education: Visva-Bharati Santiniketan, Somerville College Oxford (Left without degree), Badminton School Bristol
Died: 31 Oct 1984, Assassinated in New Delhi by bodyguards

1. Early Life - Born in Freedom Struggle

Indira was born in Nehru family - the most political family in India. Her grandfather Motilal Nehru was Congress President twice, father Jawaharlal Nehru was future PM. Her house Anand Bhavan was centre of freedom movement - Gandhi, Patel, Sarojini Naidu visited daily.

Lonely Childhood: Her mother Kamala Nehru was often ill and died in 1936 when Indira was 19. Father was always in jail or in politics. She grew up lonely. At age 4, she made a small army of 300 children called Vanar Sena (Monkey Army) in 1921 to help Congress - She made flags, gave secret messages, and collected funds. Gandhi gave her name Priyadarshini.

Education: Studied at St. Cecilia's and St. Mary's Convent Allahabad, then in Pune after mother's death. In 1934, joined Visva-Bharati Santiniketan founded by Rabindranath Tagore - Tagore named her Priyadarshini (One who looks at all with kindness). In 1937, went to Oxford University - Somerville College to study History, but left in 1941 due to WW2 and returned to India via South Africa.

2. Marriage, Freedom Struggle & Early Politics (1942-1964)

1942 - Marriage: On 26 March 1942, she married Feroze Gandhi, a Parsi journalist from Allahabad who was in youth Congress. Nehru initially opposed but later agreed. Inter-caste marriage was progressive. They had two sons Rajiv (1944) and Sanjay (1946). Marriage was not very happy - Feroze lived separately in Lucknow as MP and died in 1960 of heart attack at age 47.

1942 - Quit India: Both arrested during Quit India Movement - Indira was jailed for 9 months in Naini Jail, Allahabad.

1947-64 - As PM's Daughter: After independence, she became father's official hostess at Teen Murti Bhavan (PM House) because her mother had died. She accompanied Nehru to all foreign tours - USA, USSR, UK, China. She met world leaders like Kennedy, Eisenhower, Khrushchev. This gave her deep experience in foreign policy. She was Congress President in 1959-60 at age 42 - youngest ever. She also played role in toppling first Communist government in Kerala in 1959.

3. Rise to Prime Minister (1964-1966)

After Nehru's death on 27 May 1964, Lal Bahadur Shastri became PM. Indira became Minister of Information and Broadcasting (1964-66). She managed 1965 War media and visited battle fronts in Punjab.

After Shastri's sudden death in Tashkent on 10 Jan 1966, Congress had two candidates - Morarji Desai (senior leader) and Indira Gandhi. Congress Syndicate (K. Kamaraj, S.K. Patil) thought Indira would be weak "Goongi Gudiya" (Dumb Doll) and they could control her. She won election on 19 Jan 1966 by 355 vs 169 votes and became First Woman Prime Minister of India on 24 Jan 1966 at age 48 - Second woman PM in world after Sirimavo Bandaranaike of Sri Lanka.

Early Challenges: 1965-66 drought, food crisis, devaluation of rupee by 57% in 1966, anti-Hindi agitation, language riots. Opposition called her weak.

4. Major Decisions as Prime Minister (1966-1977) First Term

1967 - Elections & Split: Congress won 1967 election but lost many states. Syndicate tried to remove her. In 1969, President Zakir Hussain died. Syndicate candidate N. Sanjiva Reddy vs V.V. Giri (Indira's candidate). Giri won. Congress split into Congress (O) - Organization and Congress (R) - Requisitionists (Indira's). Indira became leader of real Congress with 222 MPs.

1969-71 - Socialist Reforms:
- 19 July 1969 - Nationalization of 14 Private Banks - To give banking to poor.
- 1970 - Abolition of Privy Purse - Stopped Rs 5 crore yearly payment to 560 princes.
- Garibi Hatao (Remove Poverty) slogan in 1971 election - 20 Point Programme.

1971 - Bangladesh War - Biggest Achievement:
Pakistan army killed 30 lakh Bengalis in East Pakistan. 1 crore refugees came to India. On 3 Dec 1971, Pakistan attacked India. Indira handled war brilliantly with General Sam Manekshaw. In 13 days, India defeated Pakistan, 93,000 Pakistani soldiers surrendered (largest surrender after WW2), and Bangladesh was created. On 16 Dec 1971, victory declared. Indira became Durga - Atal Bihari Vajpayee called her Durga in Parliament. She was at peak of popularity. Shimla Agreement signed in 1972 with Zulfikar Ali Bhutto.

1974 - Pokhran Nuclear Test: On 18 May 1974, India conducted first nuclear test - Smiling Buddha - at Pokhran, Rajasthan. India became 6th nuclear power.
1975 - Sikkim Merger: Sikkim merged into India as 22nd state after referendum.

5. The Emergency (1975-1977) - Most Controversial Period

On 12 June 1975, Allahabad High Court judge Jagmohanlal Sinha cancelled Indira's election from Rae Bareli in 1971 for using government machinery - case filed by Raj Narain. Supreme Court gave conditional stay.

Opposition led by Jayaprakash Narayan (JP) demanded her resignation and called for police to revolt. On 25 June 1975, on advice of Siddhartha Shankar Ray, Indira advised President Fakhruddin Ali Ahmed to declare Internal Emergency under Article 352 due to internal disturbance. Emergency declared at midnight 25 June 1975.

What Happened: Fundamental Rights suspended, Press censorship, 1 lakh opposition leaders arrested - JP, Morarji, Vajpayee, Advani. Elections postponed. Sanjay Gandhi's 5-point programme - Forced sterilization (Vasectomy), Slum demolition (Turkman Gate). Sanjay became extra-constitutional authority.
Emergency lasted 21 months till 21 March 1977. Indira said it was needed to save country from anarchy, opposition said it was to save her chair. It is considered darkest period of Indian democracy.
In 1977, she called elections. Congress lost badly - Indira herself lost Rae Bareli to Raj Narain. Janata Party came to power, Morarji Desai became PM.

6. Return to Power (1980-1984) Second Term

1977-80 - Out of Power: Janata government arrested her, but it split soon. In 1979, Charan Singh became PM for 24 days. In 1980 election, Indira returned with slogan "Indira Lao, Desh Bachao". Won 353 seats.

Second Term Challenges:
- Operation Blue Star (June 1984): Jarnail Singh Bhindranwale - Sikh militant - captured Golden Temple, Amritsar and demanded Khalistan. Indira ordered army to enter Golden Temple on 3-6 June 1984. Operation Blue Star killed Bhindranwale and 400 others, but Golden Temple was damaged - Sikhs were deeply hurt.
- Assam Movement, Punjab Militancy, LTTE in Sri Lanka started.
- She started color TV in India (Asiad 1982), brought Maruti car with Suzuki, started 6th Five Year Plan.
- Her son Sanjay Gandhi died in plane crash on 23 June 1980 near Safdarjung Airport while doing stunt - Huge personal loss. She then brought elder son Rajiv from Indian Airlines as pilot to politics in 1981.

7. Assassination & Legacy

On 31 Oct 1984 at 9:18 AM at 1, Safdarjung Road, New Delhi, two of her Sikh bodyguards - Beant Singh and Satwant Singh - fired 31 bullets to avenge Operation Blue Star. Beant Singh fired from service revolver, Satwant from Sten gun. She died at AIIMS at 2:30 PM same day. Last words - "If I die today, every drop of my blood will invigorate the nation".
After her death, anti-Sikh riots broke out in Delhi - 3000 Sikhs killed - which remains a black spot. Rajiv Gandhi became PM same evening.

Awards: Bharat Ratna (1971 after Bangladesh war), Lenin Peace Prize (1985), Bangladesh Freedom Honour (2011 posthumously).
Memorials: Indira Gandhi Memorial Museum at 1 Safdarjung Road, Shakti Sthal on Yamuna banks, Indira Gandhi International Airport Delhi.
Legacy: She was PM for 15 years 11 months (1966-77 and 1980-84) - Second longest after Nehru. She centralized power, made PMO super powerful, made India nuclear power, created Bangladesh, but Emergency is criticized. She was called Iron Lady of India - Strong, bold, never bowed to US or USSR. She said "I am not a person, I am a nation".

"Even if I died in the service of the nation, I would be proud of it. Every drop of my blood will contribute to the growth of this nation."
- Indira Gandhi, Last Speech at Bhubaneswar, 30 Oct 1984
