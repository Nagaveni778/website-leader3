Krantijyoti Savitribai Phule (1831-1897)

India's First Female Teacher | First Feminist | Social Reformer

Full Name: Savitribai Jyotirao Phule
Born: 3 Jan 1831, Naigaon, Satara District, Maharashtra
Parents: Khandoji Nevase Patil & Lakshmi Bai (Mali Community - Farmer Family)
Husband: Mahatma Jyotirao Phule (Married in 1840 at age 9)
Education: First school - at home by Jyotiba, Teacher Training - Ahmednagar Mission & Poona Normal School
Died: 10 March 1897, Pune - Died serving plague patients

1. Early Life - Child Marriage to Becoming Educated

Savitribai was born in Naigaon village in a Mali (gardener) caste, which was considered Shudra at that time. In 1840, at just 9 years old, she was married to 12-year-old Jyotirao Phule from Pune. She was illiterate at marriage - could not read or write. Jyotiba was also from Mali caste but was progressive. After marriage, Jyotiba educated Savitribai at home. He taught her Marathi and English despite opposition from his own father Govindrao who said "We are Mali, why need education?". Savitribai was very intelligent and quickly learnt. Jyotiba then sent her for formal teacher training - she went to Ms. Farar's Institution in Ahmednagar run by American missionary Cynthia Farrar (1846-47) and later to Normal School in Pune. She became the most educated woman of her time.

2. India's First Girls School - Revolution in 1848

At that time, girls were not allowed to go to school. Manusmriti said women and Shudras should not get education. Brahmin orthodoxy opposed education for all. Jyotiba and Savitribai decided to break this.

1 Jan 1848: They opened India's first girls school in Bhide Wada, Budhwar Peth, Pune at house of Tatyasaheb Bhide. This was first school for girls started by Indians (Earlier missionaries had started). Savitribai was the Headmistress - India's First Female Teacher at age 17. There were only 8 girls on first day - all from different castes.
People abused them, threw stones. Jyotiba's father threw them out of house for doing this. They had to leave home and lived with friend Usman Sheikh - Usman Sheikh gave them shelter. His sister Fatima Sheikh became Savitribai's best friend and India's first Muslim teacher.

Reaction: Orthodoxy people threw cow dung, mud, stones, eggs at Savitribai when she went to school. She carried an extra saree in her bag - after reaching school, she changed saree. This extra saree tradition became symbol of her courage.

Expansion: Within one year they opened 3 more schools:
- 3 July 1851 - Second school in Rasta Peth, Pune
- 17 girls schools total by 1852, but most closed due to lack of funds and opposition.

3. Major Social Reforms - Work Beyond Education

A) Balhatya Pratibandhak Griha (1852) - Home to Stop Infanticide:
At that time, Brahmin widows were not allowed to remarry. If they became pregnant, they killed newborn babies to avoid shame. Jyotiba and Savitribai opened first shelter home for widows to deliver babies safely. They adopted one such widow Kashibai's child - Yashwant Rao, who became their adopted son and later a doctor.

B) Fight Against Sati, Child Marriage, Caste System:
- She organized Satyashodhak Samaj weddings without dowry and Brahmin priest - first inter-caste marriage in modern India.
- She shaved heads of barbers - She organized strike of barbers against tonsuring widows. In those days, widows' heads were shaved to make them ugly. Savitribai convinced barbers in Bombay and Pune to stop this cruel practice.
- She opened Mahila Seva Mandal in 1852 to create awareness among women.
- She fought against untouchability - Opened water well for untouchables at her house - First time in Pune.

C) Satyashodhak Samaj (1873):
Jyotiba founded Satyashodhak Samaj (Truth Seekers Society) on 24 Sept 1873 to fight caste discrimination and Brahminical dominance. Savitribai was head of women's wing. She was present in all meetings, gave speeches. After Jyotiba's death in 1890, she became chairperson of Samaj.

4. Famine and Plague Service - Death as a Martyr

1876-77 & 1896 Famines: During massive famines in Maharashtra, Savitribai and Jyotiba opened free food hostels - 52 hostels in Maharashtra. They fed 2000 children daily. She said "We need food for stomach first, then education".

1897 - Bubonic Plague in Pune: This was third pandemic. Pune was worst affected. British government took no action. Savitribai and her son Dr. Yashwant opened a clinic in Hadapsar on edge of Pune. She personally carried plague patients on her back to hospital. She brought a 10-year-old boy Pandurang Babaji Gaekwad who was infected with plague from Mahar community. While carrying him, she got infected.
On 10 March 1897 at 9 PM, she died due to plague at age 66 while serving patients. She became a martyr for humanity - India's first woman to die serving plague.

5. As a Writer, Poet and Feminist

Savitribai was not just teacher - she was India's first modern Marathi poet and feminist writer. She was influenced by Thomas Clarkson's poetry.
Books:
- Kavya Phule (1854): First poetry collection - 34 poems about education, nature, caste. One of first books by a woman in India. Poem "Go, Get Education" is very famous.
- Bavan Kashi Subodh Ratnakar (1892): Second poetry book - 52 poems about history of world, written when Jyotiba died.
- She also wrote many speeches and articles in 'Satya Shodhak' newspaper.

Her Feminism: 100 years before western feminists, she said women are slaves of slaves. She fought for equal rights, widow remarriage, and said education is weapon for women liberation. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar called Jyotiba and Savitribai as his Guru.

6. Awards, Legacy & Why She Matters Today

Titles: British government honoured her as Best Teacher in 1852. Jyotiba called her Krantijyoti (Flame of Revolution).
Legacy:
- Pune University renamed as Savitribai Phule Pune University in 2014.
- Her birth anniversary 3 January is celebrated as Balika Din (Girl Child Day) in Maharashtra.
- Many schools, hostels named after her.
- She opened first school 174 years ago, but today 65 lakh girls go to school in Maharashtra because of her.
- She and Jyotiba adopted Yashwant Rao - he became doctor and served people.

Death: Cremated at same place where Jyotiba was cremated - near Pune. Both samadhis are together.
Famous for: She proved that if a 9-year-old child bride can become India's first teacher despite cow dung being thrown at her, any girl can achieve anything if given education.

Work, gather wisdom and riches"
