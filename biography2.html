Shaheed-e-Azam Bhagat Singh (1907-1931)

Revolutionary Martyr - Gave Life at Age 23

Full Name: Bhagat Singh
Born: 28 Sept 1907, Banga, Lyallpur District, Punjab (Now in Pakistan)
Parents: Kishan Singh Sandhu & Vidyavati Kaur
Family: Uncle - Ajit Singh & Swaran Singh were freedom fighters
Education: Dayanand Anglo-Vedic School Lahore, National College Lahore
Died: 23 March 1931, Lahore Central Jail (Martyred)

1. Early Life & Family Influence

Bhagat Singh was born in a Sikh Jat freedom fighter family. At the time of his birth, his father and uncles were released from jail for protesting against British Colonization Bill. His family were followers of Arya Samaj. As a child he saw Jallianwala Bagh massacre - at age 12 he went to Amritsar and collected blood-stained soil. This made him anti-British. He was deeply inspired by Kartar Singh Sarabha of Ghadar Party, who was hanged at age 19. In college he read European revolutionary movements and became an atheist and socialist. He left home at 16 to avoid marriage and joined freedom struggle.

2. Entry into Revolutionary Movement (1924-1928)

1924: Joined Hindustan Republican Association (HRA) formed by Ram Prasad Bismil, Sachindra Nath Sanyal. Later it became Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA) in 1928 with Chandrashekhar Azad as chief.
1926: Founded Naujawan Bharat Sabha to spread revolutionary ideas among youth and promote Hindu-Muslim unity.
1927: First arrest for Lahore Bomb Case but released later.
Motto: HSRA's motto was to make India socialist and free from British through armed revolution, not Gandhian non-violence. Their slogan was "Inquilab Zindabad" which Bhagat Singh popularized.

3. Major Revolutionary Actions

1928 - Killing of John Saunders to avenge Lala Lajpat Rai:
On 30 Oct 1928, Lala Lajpat Rai was lathi-charged by British Superintendent James A. Scott during protest against Simon Commission. He died on 17 Nov. To avenge his death, Bhagat Singh, Rajguru and Azad decided to kill Scott. But on 17 Dec 1928, they mistakenly shot and killed Assistant Superintendent John P. Saunders in Lahore. They escaped by cutting beard and changing identity. Posters said - "Saunders is dead, Lalaji is avenged."

1929 - Central Assembly Bombing - To Make the Deaf Hear:
To protest against Public Safety Bill and Trade Disputes Bill that curtailed civil rights, HSRA decided to bomb Central Legislative Assembly in Delhi without killing anyone. On 8 April 1929, Bhagat Singh and Batukeshwar Dutt threw low-intensity bombs in empty benches of Assembly and shouted "Inquilab Zindabad" and threw leaflets. They purposely did not run away and were arrested. He said - "We want to make the deaf hear."

1929-1930 - Lahore Conspiracy Case & Hunger Strike:
In jail, Bhagat Singh and comrades started 116-day hunger strike for better conditions for political prisoners. British treated Indian prisoners worse than British prisoners. During this, Jatindra Nath Das died after 63-day hunger strike on 13 Sept 1929. This shook whole country.

4. Jail, Ideology & Writings

In jail for 2 years, Bhagat Singh studied intensively - he read Lenin, Marx, Bakunin, and was fluent in English, Hindi, Punjabi, Urdu, Bengali. He wrote 4 books in jail.

Why I am an Atheist (1930): Written in Lahore Jail, explaining he became atheist due to reason and revolutionary thinking.
Jail Notebook: Contains quotes from hundreds of authors about socialism and revolution.
His Vision: He was not just a nationalist but a socialist. He wanted freedom from poverty, inequality, and capitalism, not just from British. He wanted to remove communalism and casteism.
He shaved his beard and cut hair for revolution - which was a big sacrifice for a Sikh at that time.

5. Trial, Execution & Martyrdom

British formed a special tribunal and sentenced Bhagat Singh, Rajguru and Sukhdev to death in Lahore Conspiracy Case.
Many leaders including Gandhiji and Congress appealed for mercy but Viceroy Irwin rejected.

23 March 1931: One day before scheduled date (24 March), British feared public protest, so they secretly hanged all three at 7:30 PM in Lahore Central Jail. They were just 23 (Bhagat Singh), 22 (Rajguru) and 23 (Sukhdev) years old.
Jail authorities broke the back wall of jail and secretly cremated them on banks of Sutlej river at Hussainiwala (now in Firozpur). Villagers saw fire and gathered. Next day people found their half-burnt remains.
His death created huge anger - Hartals, protests across India. Gandhiji was criticized for not saving him.

6. Books, Slogan & Legacy

Popular Slogans: Inquilab Zindabad (Long Live Revolution), Sarfaroshi ki tamanna ab hamare dil me hai.
Comrades: Chandrashekhar Azad, Rajguru, Sukhdev, Batukeshwar Dutt, Jatindra Nath Das, Durga Bhabhi.
Films & Memorials: National Martyrs Memorial at Hussainiwala (India-Pakistan border), Shaheed Bhagat Singh Museum at Khatkar Kalan (his ancestral village), and Parliament House statue.
Legacy: He became the youth icon of India. Every year 23 March is celebrated as Shaheed Diwas (Martyrs' Day) and 28 Sept as his birth anniversary. His ideology inspired Indian socialist movement and left politics.
He is called Shaheed-e-Azam (Great Martyr) - the only person in Indian history given this title.

"They can kill me, but they cannot kill my ideas. They can crush my body, but they will not be able to crush my spirit."
- Bhagat Singh, in Jail Notebook
