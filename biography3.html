Dr. APJ Abdul Kalam (1931-2015)

Missile Man of India | People's President | Bharat Ratna

Full Name: Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam
Born: 15 Oct 1931, Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu
Parents: Jainulabdeen Marakayar (Boat owner) & Ashiamma Jainulabdeen
Family: Youngest of 5 siblings - 4 brothers, 1 sister
Education: Rameswaram Elementary, Schwartz High School, St. Joseph's College Trichy, MIT - Madras Institute of Technology
Died: 27 July 2015, Shillong, Meghalaya (While giving lecture)

1. Early Life & Struggle in Rameswaram

Kalam was born in a poor Tamil Muslim family in the pilgrim town of Rameswaram. His father owned a boat and was Imam of mosque. His family was not rich - they lived in a small ancestral house near Rameswaram temple. To support family, young Kalam did many jobs - at age 8, after 2nd World War started, there was shortage of tamarind seeds. He collected seeds and sold them. He also distributed newspapers after school to earn money. He was average in studies but very hardworking and loved mathematics. His teachers Iyadurai Solomon and Sivasubramania Iyer inspired him. Iyer, a Brahmin teacher, invited Kalam to his home for lunch, defying caste barriers - this shaped his secular thinking. His sister Zohara pawned her gold bangles to pay his college fees of Rs 600 for MIT.

2. Education - From Rameswaram to MIT (1950-1960)

1950: Joined St. Joseph's College, Trichy for BSc Physics. He wanted to become fighter pilot but was poor in English.
1955: Joined Madras Institute of Technology (MIT), Chrompet - studied Aerospace Engineering. He was called "Theodore von Karman of MIT". Worked 18 hours a day on projects. One incident - his professor Dr. Srinivasan rejected his aerodynamics project and gave 3 days to redesign. Kalam worked day and night and impressed professor.
1958: Dream of becoming Air Force pilot - Went to Dehradun for Air Force selection. Stood 9th in interview, but only 8 seats were there. He missed by one rank. He was deeply depressed and went to Rishikesh. He met Swami Sivananda who told him "Desire, when it is destiny, will come true."
1960: Graduated as Aeronautical Engineer and joined DRDO as junior scientist with Rs 250 salary.

3. Scientist Career - ISRO & DRDO (1960-1992)

1960-1969 - DRDO, Delhi: Started career at Defence Institute of Advanced Technology, Pune. Worked on hovercraft prototype - first hovercraft in India - called Nandi. But DRDO was not interested, so he left.

1969-1982 - ISRO, The Golden Period: Joined ISRO on advice of Vikram Sarabhai, father of Indian Space. He was Project Director of India's first satellite launch vehicle.
- SLV-III (1979-80): First satellite launch vehicle. First attempt in 1979 failed - satellite fell into Bay of Bengal. Kalam took responsibility publicly. Chairman Satish Dhawan said "We will try again". On 18 July 1980, SLV-III launched Rohini satellite successfully - India became 6th country to launch satellite. He became national hero.
- Worked with Dr. Brahm Prakash and developed composite materials for rockets.

1982-1992 - DRDO - Missile Man: Rejoined DRDO as Director, Defence Research and Development Laboratory (DRDL), Hyderabad. Started Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme (IGMDP) on request of Indira Gandhi and R. Venkataraman. Developed 5 missiles:
1. Prithvi - Surface to surface short range (1988)
2. Agni - Intermediate range ballistic missile (1989) - ICBM
3. Akash - Surface to air
4. Nag - Anti-tank
5. Trishul - Short range surface to air
Because of this he was called Missile Man of India. Worked closely with Dr. Arunachalam.

1992-1999 - Chief Scientific Advisor: Scientific Advisor to Defence Minister and Secretary DRDO. Played key role in Pokhran-II Nuclear Tests (11 & 13 May 1998) with R. Chidambaram and Anil Kakodkar. India became nuclear power. He was the chief coordinator. After test, Atal Bihari Vajpayee declared India as nuclear state.

4. People's President (2002-2007) - 11th President of India

In 2002, NDA government led by Vajpayee proposed his name for President. He was supported by both BJP and Congress. He won with 90% votes - became 11th President on 25 July 2002. He was called People's President because:

- He opened Rashtrapati Bhavan to common people, students.
- He visited every state, every institution - He visited 500+ schools and colleges as President.
- He lived very simply - came with 2 suitcases and left with 2 suitcases.
- He never signed mercy petitions without full thought - He signed Office of Profit Bill.
- He was first President to travel in submarine (INS Sindhurakshak), fighter jet Sukhoi-30MKI, and to address European Parliament.
- His address "I am not a man of my party, I am a man of my country" was famous.
His tenure ended on 25 July 2007. He wanted to contest again but later withdrew.

5. Post Presidency - Teacher Till Last Breath (2007-2015)

After presidency, he did not take pension, did not take government bungalow in Delhi. He became Visiting Professor at IIM Shillong, IIM Ahmedabad, IIM Indore, IISc Bangalore, Anna University. He launched missions:
PURA - Providing Urban Amenities to Rural Areas (2003): His vision to develop villages with city facilities.
Vision 2020: In his book India 2020 (1998), he gave roadmap to make India developed nation by 2020 - focusing on agriculture, healthcare, education, technology.
Kalam's 10 Oaths for Youth.

On 27 July 2015, while delivering lecture on "Creating a Livable Planet Earth" at IIM Shillong, he collapsed on stage at 6:30 PM due to cardiac arrest. He died at age 83. His last words to his aide Srijan Pal Singh were "Funny guy! Are you doing well?" He was buried with full state honours in Rameswaram. His last book "Transcendence" was released after death.

6. Awards, Books & Personal Life

Awards: He got 48 honorary doctorates. Padma Bhushan (1981), Padma Vibhushan (1990), Bharat Ratna (1997) - Highest civilian award. King Charles II Medal (UK), Von Braun Award (USA). India named its missile test site "APJ Abdul Kalam Island" (Wheeler Island).
Books: Wings of Fire (Autobiography - Most famous, translated in 18 languages), Ignited Minds, India 2020, My Journey, Turning Points, Target 3 Billion.
Personal Life: He never married - remained bachelor. He was vegetarian (although born Muslim), read both Quran and Bhagavad Gita daily. Played Veena, wrote Tamil poetry. His daily routine - woke at 4 AM, studied till 6, then walk. He owned very little - few books, veena, 2-3 suits, one watch. He donated all his wealth to trust.
Salary: As President he donated half salary to charity.
Legacy: Every year 15 Oct is World Students Day by UN in his honour. His memorial in Rameswaram is designed by ISRO - shows missiles and veena.
