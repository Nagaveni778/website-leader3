Sarojini Naidu (1879-1949)

Nightingale of India | First Woman President of Congress | First Woman Governor

Full Name: Sarojini Chattopadhyay Naidu
Born: 13 Feb 1879, Hyderabad, Telangana (Then Nizam State)
Parents: Aghorenath Chattopadhyay (Scientist & Principal) & Barada Sundari Devi (Poetess)
Husband: Dr. Govindarajulu Naidu (Married 1898 - Inter-caste love marriage)
Children: 5 - Jayasurya, Padmaja, Randheer, Leelamani, Nilawar
Education: Madras University, King's College London, Girton College Cambridge
Died: 2 March 1949, Lucknow, UP - While in office as Governor

1. Early Life - Child Prodigy Born in Genius Family

Sarojini was born in a Bengali Brahmin family settled in Hyderabad. Her family was full of scholars. Her father Aghorenath Chattopadhyay was a scientist who got DSc from Edinburgh University - first Indian to get DSc, he was Principal of Nizam College Hyderabad and alchemist. Her mother Barada Sundari was a Bengali poetess who wrote poems in Bengali. Her brother Virendranath was famous revolutionary, Harindranath was poet and actor, Sunalini was dancer.

From childhood, Sarojini was genius. At age 12, she topped Matriculation in Madras Presidency. At age 13, she wrote a 2000-line poem "Lady of the Lake" in English. At 14, she wrote 1300-line play "Maher Muneer" in Persian/Urdu style which impressed Nizam of Hyderabad. Nizam Mir Mahbub Ali Khan gave her scholarship of Rs 3000 to study in England when she was 16. She was multilingual - knew English, Bengali, Urdu, Telugu, Persian, Sanskrit. At age 12, she also wrote about algebra problems that surprised her father. She wanted to be scientist like father but became poet.

2. Education in England & Love Marriage (1895-1898)

1895 - England: At 16, she went to King's College London and then Girton College, Cambridge with scholarship. She met Edmund Gosse and Arthur Symons - famous English critics who advised her to write about Indian themes, not English themes. Symons said "Your Indian soul is your strength". She was deeply homesick and ill in England.

Love Story: In Hyderabad, before going to England, at age 15 she fell in love with Dr. Govindarajulu Naidu - a South Indian non-Brahmin doctor from Andhra, 10 years elder, who was already a widower. Inter-caste, inter-region love marriage was unthinkable in 1890s. Her father initially opposed but later agreed because Govindarajulu was progressive and doctor. They married in 1898 under Brahmo Samaj Act - First inter-caste love marriage of that time that created sensation in Madras. It was successful - They had 5 children. Her daughter Padmaja Naidu later became Governor of West Bengal.

She returned to Hyderabad in 1898 and settled there.

3. Nightingale of India - Poet Career (1905-1917)

Sarojini started writing poetry at age 13 and became famous as poet before she became freedom fighter. She wrote in English but with Indian soul - about palanquin bearers, bangles sellers, snake charmers, bazaars of Hyderabad.
Three Famous Books:
1. The Golden Threshold (1905): Published in London with introduction by Arthur Symons - Became bestseller. Contains poem "Palanquin Bearers" - "Lightly, O lightly we bear her along".
2. The Bird of Time (1912): Contains famous poems "In the Bazaars of Hyderabad" - "What do you sell, O ye merchants?" Still taught in schools.
3. The Broken Wing (1917): Dedicated to Muhammad Ali Jinnah - It has more patriotic poems.

Mahatma Gandhi called her "Bharat Kokila - Nightingale of India" in 1919 because of her sweet voice, poetry and singing style of speech. Jawaharlal Nehru said her speech was like music. Edmund Gosse said she is "Most gifted woman of her generation". Her poems are romantic, lyrical, and about Indian culture, not western. She wrote 150+ poems. She also wrote plays.

4. Freedom Struggle - From Poet to Politician (1905-1947)

1905 - Entry: Met Gopal Krishna Gokhale in Calcutta - He told her "Use your poetry and voice for nation, not just for art". She joined Indian National Congress in 1905 after Bengal partition.

1914-1918 - Meeting Gandhi: Met Gandhi in London in 1914 while he was returning from South Africa. She became his close follower. She helped him in Champaran.

1917 - Women's Movement: She went to London with Annie Besant and presented demand for women's voting rights to British Parliament. She founded Women's Indian Association in 1917. She said "Women are equal partners of men in nation building".

1919 - Rowlatt Act & Jallianwala Bagh: She gave fiery speeches against Rowlatt Act, led protests in Bombay.

1920-22 - Non-Cooperation: She toured all over India with Gandhi, gave speeches in English, Hindi, Bengali - Crowd loved her oratory. Arrested many times.

1925 - First Woman President of Congress: In Kanpur Session 1925, she became first Indian woman President of Indian National Congress - Second woman after Annie Besant. At age 46, she presided over Congress.

1930 - Dandi March & Arrest: Gandhi chose her as one of first women to join Dandi March to make salt. After Gandhi's arrest on 5 May 1930, she led the raid on Dharasana Salt Works (25 km south of Dandi) with 2000 volunteers. British beat them brutally - American journalist Webb Miller reported it worldwide. She was arrested and jailed for 8 months in Yerwada Jail.

1931 - Round Table Conference: Went with Gandhi to London as woman representative.

1932 & 1942 - Jail: Arrested in Civil Disobedience and Quit India Movement. In 1942, she was arrested with Gandhi and kept in Aga Khan Palace Jail, Pune for 21 months with Kasturba and Gandhi.

1942-45 - Hindu-Muslim Unity: She was known for Hindu-Muslim unity. Jinnah called her "Ambassador of Hindu-Muslim Unity". She tried to stop partition till last.

5. First Woman Governor of India (1947-1949)

After Independence on 15 Aug 1947, Nehru wanted Sarojini as minister but Gandhi said "Make her Governor". On 15 Aug 1947, she became First Woman Governor of United Provinces (Now Uttar Pradesh) - First woman governor in India and in British Commonwealth. She lived in Lucknow Raj Bhavan. She was governor till death. She was very popular - She opened Raj Bhavan for poor, poets, students. She said "I am not a ruler, I am a mother". She died in office.

6. Death, Books, Legacy & Interesting Facts

Death: On 2 March 1949, at age 70, she died of heart attack in Lucknow Government House while working as Governor. She was writing a letter till last day. She was buried in Hyderabad. Nehru said "She was a great poet and great politician - rare combination".

Awards: Kaisar-i-Hind Medal by British in 1911 (which she returned after Jallianwala Bagh in 1919), Honorary doctorate from many universities.
Famous Poems Every Child Reads: Palanquin Bearers, In The Bazaars of Hyderabad, Coromandel Fishers, Indian Weavers - "Weavers, weaving at break of day, why do you weave a garment so gay?"
Personality: She was witty - She called Gandhi "Mickey Mouse" jokingly, and Gandhi called her "Bharat Kokila". She could give speech for 2 hours without paper in 5 languages. She smoked cigarettes - rare for women then, and said "Smoking helps me think poetry".
Legacy: Her birthday 13 Feb is celebrated as National Women's Day in India and World Radio Day. Sarojini Naidu Medical College Agra, Sarojini Naidu Vanita Maha Vidyalaya Hyderabad, Many roads named. Her house "The Golden Threshold" in Hyderabad is now museum and part of Hyderabad University.
Children: Padmaja Naidu was Governor of West Bengal (1956-67) and close to Nehru, Jayasurya was freedom fighter.

She proved women can be poet, orator, politician, governor and mother at same time.

"We want deeper sincerity of motive, a greater courage in speech and earnestness in action."
- Sarojini Naidu, Speech at Bombay 1918
