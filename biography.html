Mahatma Gandhi (1869-1948)

Father of the Nation | Bapu | Satya & Ahimsa

Full Name: Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi
Born: 2 Oct 1869, Porbandar, Gujarat
Parents: Karamchand Uttamchand Gandhi (Diwan of Porbandar) & Putlibai
Wife: Kasturba Gandhi (Married 1883 at age 13)
Children: 4 Sons - Harilal, Manilal, Ramdas, Devadas
Education: Law - University College London, Inner Temple London
Died: 30 Jan 1948, Assassinated at Birla House, New Delhi by Nathuram Godse

1. Early Life & Childhood (1869-1888)

Mohandas was born in a middle-class Modh Bania family in Porbandar coastal town. His father Karamchand was Diwan (Chief Minister) of Porbandar, Rajkot and Wankaner. His mother Putlibai was deeply religious - she fasted and prayed daily. Gandhi learnt truth and vegetarianism from her. He was average in studies, shy, and afraid of darkness and thieves.

At age 7, family shifted to Rajkot. At age 13, he was married to 14-year-old Kasturba Makhanji Kapadia - child marriage as per custom. He was jealous husband initially. He once ate meat secretly with friend Sheikh Mehtab to become strong like British, but could not swallow and later vowed to be pure vegetarian forever.
At age 15, he stole gold from his brother's armlet to pay debt of his brother. He confessed to father on paper and father cried - This taught him power of confession and Satya. His father died when Gandhi was 16, when he was with Kasturba - He always regretted he was not near father at death time.
He passed Matriculation in 1887 and joined Samaldas College Bhavnagar but left and went to London to become barrister in 1888 at age 18, leaving wife and 1-year-old son Harilal.

2. London & South Africa - Making of Mahatma (1888-1915)

1888-1891 London: In London, he studied law at Inner Temple. He struggled - wore English clothes, tried to become English gentleman, took dance lessons. But soon he became vegetarian activist - Joined London Vegetarian Society. He read Bhagavad Gita in English translation for first time in London which changed his life. He read Bible, Quran also. He passed barrister exam on 10 June 1891 and returned to India.

1891-93 India Failure: Started law practice in Rajkot and Bombay but failed as lawyer - Too shy to cross-examine witnesses. Could not earn. Got offer from Dada Abdullah Company to go to South Africa as lawyer for 1 year for £105.

1893-1914 South Africa - Turning Point: On 7 June 1893 in Pietermaritzburg station, South Africa, Gandhi was thrown out of first-class train despite having ticket because he was brown Indian - Whites only. He spent night in cold waiting room - This insult changed his life - He decided to fight racial injustice.
He fought for Indians (Called coolies) in South Africa. In 1894, founded Natal Indian Congress. In 1906, started first Satyagraha against compulsory registration of Indians - He was jailed for first time. In 1908, burnt registration cards. In 1910, founded Tolstoy Farm near Johannesburg with help of Hermann Kallenbach. In 1913, led march of 2000 Indians against poll tax on Indians. He was jailed 4 times in South Africa. He developed weapons - Satyagraha (Truth force) and Ahimsa (Non-violence). South African government finally compromised in 1914 - Smuts-Gandhi Agreement signed. He returned to India in Jan 1915 as hero. In South Africa, he wore suit, later wore Kathiawadi dress, then finally loin cloth.

3. Entry in Indian Freedom Struggle (1915-1922)

On return, Gopal Krishna Gokhale became his political guru and said "Travel India for 1 year and see". Gandhi travelled all over India in 3rd class train.
1917 - First Satyagraha in India - Champaran, Bihar: Farmers were forced to grow indigo (Neel) for British planters. Gandhi went to Champaran, was arrested, but finally British abolished Tinkathia system. Success.
1918 - Kheda & Ahmedabad: Kheda farmers satyagraha for tax relief after flood, and Ahmedabad mill workers strike for 35% wage hike - Gandhi did first hunger strike in India for workers.
1919 - Rowlatt Act & Jallianwala Bagh: British passed Rowlatt Act to jail Indians without trial. Gandhi called for all-India hartal on 6 April 1919. On 13 April 1919, General Dyer killed 379 Indians in Jallianwala Bagh, Amritsar. Gandhi was shocked.
1920-22 - Non-Cooperation Movement: After Khilafat issue and Punjab wrongs, Gandhi launched biggest movement - Boycott British schools, courts, cloth, titles. People burnt foreign clothes. Thousands students left colleges, lawyers like Motilal Nehru left practice. He said "Swaraj in one year". But on 5 Feb 1922, in Chauri Chaura, UP, mob burnt 22 policemen alive in police station. Gandhi was deeply hurt by violence and suddenly withdrew movement. Nehru and youth were angry, but Gandhi said non-violence is non-negotiable. British arrested him on 10 March 1922 and gave 6 years jail in Yerwada. He was released in 1924 after appendicitis operation.

4. Middle Years - Salt Satyagraha & Major Movements (1924-1939)

1924-28 - Constructive Work: After jail, Gandhi did Khadi promotion, removal of untouchability, Hindu-Muslim unity, village work at Sabarmati Ashram (founded 1915) and later Wardha.

1930-31 - Civil Disobedience & Dandi March - Most Famous: On 12 March 1930, at age 61, Gandhi started 385 km march from Sabarmati Ashram to Dandi sea beach with 78 followers to make salt and break British salt law - British had monopoly on salt and taxed it. He walked 24 days. On 6 April 1930, he picked salt at Dandi beach and said "With this salt, I am shaking foundations of British Empire". 60,000 people arrested including Gandhi, Nehru, Patel. British beat protesters at Dharasana Salt Works. World media covered.
