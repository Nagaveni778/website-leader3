Netaji Subhash Chandra Bose (1897-1945)

Founder of Azad Hind Fauj | Gave Slogan Jai Hind

Full Name: Subhash Chandra Bose
Born: 23 Jan 1897, Cuttack, Odisha (Then Bengal Presidency)
Parents: Janakinath Bose (Lawyer) & Prabhavati Devi
Family: 9th child of 14 children
Wife: Emilie Schenkl (Austrian) - Secret marriage in 1937
Daughter: Anita Bose Pfaff (Born 1942, Germany)
Education: Ravenshaw Collegiate Cuttack, Presidency College Calcutta, Cambridge University (ICS)
Death: 18 Aug 1945 - Disputed (Taipei Plane Crash)

1. Early Life & Brilliant Student (1897-1919)

Subhash was born in a middle-class Bengali family in Cuttack. His father Janakinath Bose was a famous lawyer and got title of Rai Bahadur from British, but later left it. His mother was deeply religious. From childhood, Subhash was patriotic and spiritual - he was influenced by teachings of Ramakrishna Paramahamsa and Swami Vivekananda. He said "Service to mankind is service to God".

He studied at Protestant European School (now Stewart School) and Ravenshaw Collegiate School. He topped Matriculation in Cuttack in 1913. In 1915, in Presidency College Calcutta, he beat Professor E.F. Oaten who insulted Indians - he was rusticated from college. This was his first revolt. He then joined Scottish Church College and topped with First Class in Philosophy in 1919. His father wanted him to become ICS (Indian Civil Service) - the highest British job. He went to England in 1919 to study at Cambridge.

2. ICS to Freedom Fighter (1920-1921)

1920 - Topped ICS: In England, he prepared in just 8 months and secured 4th rank in ICS exam - one of toughest exams. He was selected.

1921 - Resigned from ICS: He could have lived a luxurious life with 1000 Pounds salary. But Jallianwala Bagh massacre and Gandhiji's call disturbed him. He wrote letter to his brother Sarat - "I will not serve British Government that killed my people". On 22 April 1921, he resigned from ICS - first Indian to resign from ICS for freedom. British were shocked. He returned to India in July 1921 and met Gandhiji in Bombay. Gandhiji advised him to join Chittaranjan Das (Deshbandhu), the leader of Bengal.

He joined Indian National Congress and became Principal of National College, Calcutta founded by Das.

3. Congress Leader & 11 Times Jail (1921-1939)

1921-22: Participated in Non-Cooperation Movement, led youth boycott of Prince of Wales visit. Arrested first time on 10 Dec 1921 for 6 months.
1924: Elected CEO of Calcutta Municipal Corporation by C.R. Das. Improved roads and education. Arrested again and sent to Mandalay Jail, Burma (Myanmar) for 3 years without trial - he got tuberculosis there.
1928: Organized Congress session in Calcutta with full military discipline as General Officer Commanding - wore general uniform.
1930: Elected Mayor of Calcutta at age 33 - youngest mayor. Arrested for Dandi March.
1930-1933: In jail, he wrote his book "The Indian Struggle". British banned him from entering India for 6 years, he lived in Europe.
1938 & 1939 - President of Indian National Congress: Elected President of Haripura Session (1938) and Tripuri Session (1939). In Haripura, he brought first Planning Committee with Nehru as head - idea of Planning Commission came from him. In 1939, he defeated Pattabhi Sitaramayya who was supported by Gandhi. Gandhi said "Pattabhi's defeat is my defeat". Due to differences with Gandhi on non-violence and negotiation with British, Bose resigned from Congress on 29 April 1939.
1939 - Forward Bloc: On 3 May 1939, he formed Forward Bloc - a left-wing party to unite all socialists and bring armed struggle. He was arrested again on 2 July 1940 under Defence of India Act and kept in Presidency Jail.

4. The Great Escape - From Calcutta to Germany (1940-1941)

This is the most filmy escape in Indian history. In jail, Bose did hunger strike for 7 days demanding release. British feared he would die and released him to house arrest in his Elgin Road house, Calcutta (now Netaji Bhawan).

On 16-17 Jan 1941, at 1:30 AM, he escaped from house arrest dressed as Pathan named Mohammad Ziauddin. He had grown beard, wore long salwar and turban. His nephew Sisir Kumar Bose drove him in Wanderer car (Car number BLA 7169 - still kept in museum). British police thought he is inside house for 14 days - he kept beard pillow under blanket.

Route: Calcutta -> Dhanbad -> Gomoh -> Delhi -> Peshawar -> Kabul (Afghanistan) -> Moscow -> Berlin, Germany.
In Kabul, he stayed with Uttam Chand Malhotra. With help of Abwehr (German intelligence) and with Italian passport of Orlando Mazzotta, he reached Berlin on 3 April 1941 via Soviet Union. In Germany he was welcomed by Hitler, but he did not like Hitler's racism.

5. In Germany & Formation of Azad Hind Fauj - Part 1

1941-1943 in Germany: In Berlin, Bose formed Free India Centre and Azad Hind Radio - he broadcast daily from Berlin in English, Hindi, Tamil. He formed Indian Legion (Indische Legion) of 4500 Indian POWs captured by Germany - They took oath to fight for India. He was given title of Netaji by German Indian soldiers. Here he met his wife Emilie Schenkl who worked as his secretary and translator. They married secretly in 1937 in Germany and had daughter Anita in Nov 1942.
He wanted to attack India from Germany but Hitler refused to help. Japan was winning in Asia, so Bose decided to go to Japan. German submarine U-180 took him on most dangerous 90-day submarine journey - Only one Indian to travel in submarine in WW2.
Submarine Journey (Feb - May 1943): From Kiel, Germany to Sabang Island near Sumatra. Mid-ocean, he transferred to Japanese submarine I-29. Reached Tokyo on 16 May 1943 and then Singapore on 2 July 1943.

6. Azad Hind Fauj & Provisional Government (1943-1945)

4 July 1943: In Singapore, Rash Behari Bose (Old revolutionary) handed over leadership of Indian Independence League and Indian National Army (Azad Hind Fauj) to Subhash Bose. INA was first formed in 1942 with 40,000 Indian soldiers of British Indian Army captured by Japan.
5 July 1943: Netaji became Supreme Commander of Azad Hind Fauj. He gave battle cry "Chalo Delhi" and "Jai Hind" - Jai Hind later became national slogan of India. He gave slogan "Tum mujhe khoon do, main tumhe azadi dunga" (Give me blood, I will give you freedom).
21 Oct 1943: Formed Provisional Government of Azad Hind (Azad Hind Sarkar) in Singapore - First independent government of India. It was recognized by 9 countries including Japan, Germany, Italy, Burma. He declared war on Britain and USA.
INA Army: 60,000 soldiers including 1000 women - Rani of Jhansi Regiment led by Captain Lakshmi Sahgal - First women's regiment in Asia. Hindu, Muslim, Sikh all together - Bose removed caste, religion.
30 Dec 1943: Netaji hoisted Indian Tricolour for first time on Indian soil in Andaman & Nicobar Islands. He renamed Andaman as Shaheed Island and Nicobar as Swaraj Island. He set up INA headquarters in Port Blair.

1944 - Imphal & Kohima War - Do or Die: With slogan "Chalo Delhi", INA with Japanese army attacked British India from Burma side. They captured Moirang (Manipur) on 14 April 1944 - First Indian town liberated. They besieged Imphal and Kohima - Battle of Imphal is called Britain's toughest battle in WW2. But monsoon, no air support, no ration led to defeat. 10,000 INA soldiers died. Bose retreated but said "I will come back".
He established Azad Hind Bank and issued currency.

7. Mystery Death & Legacy

18 Aug 1945 - Alleged Death: After Japan surrendered, Bose tried to go to Russia for help. He boarded Japanese bomber from Taihoku (Taipei) to Manchuria. Plane crashed at 2 PM after takeoff - Japanese report says Bose got 3rd-degree burns. He died at 9 PM same night in Taihoku hospital. But many Indians do not believe this. Three commissions - Shah Nawaz (1956), Khosla (1970), Mukherjee Commission (2006) investigated - Mukherjee Commission said no plane crash happened that day. His ashes are still kept at Renkoji Temple, Tokyo. Gumnami Baba theory also famous in Faizabad.

Legacy:
- His INA soldiers trial in Red Fort in 1945-46 created huge nationalist wave - All Indians united against British, which led to British leaving India.
- His birthday 23 Jan is celebrated as Parakram Diwas (Day of Valour) from 2021.
- Netaji Subhash Chandra Bose International Airport, Kolkata named after him.
- His cap (Netaji cap) and INA uniform are iconic.
- He was secular - He had AN Ahmadi Muslim friend Abid Hasan who cooked for him, and his driver was Muslim.
- He wrote books "The Indian Struggle" and "An Indian Pilgrim".
- He gave India its national greeting - JAI HIND.

"Give me blood and I will give you freedom!"
- Netaji Subhash Chandra Bose, Burma, 1944
