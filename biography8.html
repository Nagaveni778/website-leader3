Kittur Rani Chennamma (1778-1829)

First Woman to Fight British | 33 Years Before Jhansi Rani

Full Name: Chennamma Desai
Born: 23 Oct 1778, Kakati Village, Belagavi District, Karnataka
Parents: Dhulappa Desai (Chief of Kakati) & Padmavati
Husband: Raja Mallasarja Desai, King of Kittur (Married 1793 at age 15)
Son: Rajaguru (Shivalingappa) - Biological son died in 1824, Adopted Shivalingappa
Kingdom: Kittur - 300 villages, Area - Now Belagavi, Dharwad region
Died: 21 Feb 1829, Bailhongal Jail, Karnataka - Died in British prison

1. Early Life - Born as Warrior Princess

Chennamma was born in Kakati, a small village near Belagavi, in Lingayat community (Veerashaiva). Her father Dhulappa Desai was chief of Kakati and trained her like a son. Unlike other girls, she learnt from childhood horse riding, sword fighting, archery, wrestling (Kusti) and Kalaripayattu. She could shoot arrow on moving target, lift 40 kg sword with one hand. She was also expert in scriptures - Kannada, Sanskrit, Marathi and Urdu. People called her "Kakati Chennamma" for her bravery. At age 15, in 1793, she was married to Raja Mallasarja Desai, the ruler of Kittur. Kittur was one of most prosperous kingdoms in North Karnataka - known for gold, cotton and brave army. It was ruled by Desai family since 1586. Mallasarja was already married to Rudramma, but had no child, so second marriage with Chennamma.

2. Queen of Kittur & Family Tragedies (1793-1824)

1793-1816 - Happy Life: After marriage, Chennamma became queen of Kittur. Mallasarja was a just ruler - He built many temples, tanks, forts. Chennamma assisted him in administration and also led women's army. She was loved by people because she solved disputes personally - She sat in court daily.

1816 - Husband's Death: Raja Mallasarja died in 1816 due to illness, leaving huge treasury and no heir. Their only biological son had died earlier. Kittur was managed by Chennamma as Regent with help of minister and husband's son from first wife - Shivaling Rudra Sarja who was acting king.

1824 - Second Tragedy: On 20 June 1824, Shivaling Rudra Sarja, who was acting ruler and son of Mallasarja's first wife, died without child. Now Kittur had no ruler at all. Rani Chennamma, aged 46, was the only authority. She had to save kingdom from British.

3. Doctrine of Lapse Before Dalhousie - British Plot

Long before Lord Dalhousie made Doctrine of Lapse official in 1848, British in Bombay Presidency were already using it. Collector of Dharwad - Mr. Thackeray (Father of famous English writer William Thackeray) and Mr. Chaplin, Commissioner, wanted to capture Kittur because it was rich - Yearly revenue Rs 5 lakh (huge in 1824).

Adoption: Rani Chennamma adopted a boy named Shivalingappa (Shivalingappa was son of her relative - 8-year-old) on 21 Oct 1824 and made him heir. She informed British through letter with help of lawyer. She argued adoption is allowed by Hindu Shastra.
British Rejection: Thackeray rejected adoption saying "Kittur is dependent on British, so British permission is needed for adoption". He sent letter - "Vacate Kittur, British will take over". He demanded surrender of treasury and jewels worth Rs 15 lakh. Rani replied: "Kittur is not British property, it is my husband's property. I will not surrender."
Thackeray threatened war. Rani prepared for war - Called her Sardars - Gurusiddappa, Amatur Balappa, and Diwan Mallappa Shetty. She collected 8000 soldiers, 500 horses, and 20 cannons.

4. First War Against British - Victory (23 Oct 1824)

This is historic - First war won by an Indian woman against British - 33 years before Rani Lakshmibai.

On 21 Oct 1824, British army of 20,000 soldiers with 2 cannons under Thackeray, Sub-Collector Munro (nephew of Sir Thomas Munro, Governor of Madras) attacked Kittur Fort. Fort had only 8000 soldiers but was strong with 3-layer walls and 6 gates.

23 Oct 1824 - Battle of Kittur: At 10 AM, battle started near Kittur Fort. Rani herself rode white horse, wearing armour, holding two swords, with adopted son tied on back (like Lakshmibai later). Her commander Amatur Balappa - a great swordsman - killed Thackeray with his sword - Thackeray died on spot. Munro also died - hit by bullet. British army panicked and fled. 500 British soldiers were killed, 400 captured.

Rani won first battle. She released British prisoners and sent a letter to Chaplin - "Return our kingdom, stop war". Chaplin was angry and called more army from Bombay. British thought defeat by a woman was insult to British Empire. Governor of Bombay - Mountstuart Elphinstone sent General Deacon with huge army.

Rani's Guru Sangolli Rayanna - a poor shepherd soldier - became her chief commander. He used guerrilla warfare - He hid in forests and attacked British supply lines. He is still folk hero in Karnataka.

5. Second War - Defeat and Imprisonment (3 Dec 1824 - 1829)

After defeat, British brought massive army - 3 regiments, 10 cannons, 5000 horses under Chaplin and Deacon from Belagavi, Sholapur and Dharwad. Total 50,000 soldiers.
3 Dec 1824 - Second Battle: Fort was besieged for 21 days. Rani's army fought bravely but ran out of gunpowder. Betrayal happened - Mallappa Shetty and Venkata Rao - two officers - mixed mud and cow dung in gunpowder and opened fort gate for British for money. British entered fort.

Rani Chennamma, adopted son Shivalingappa and Sangolli Rayanna were captured. Kittur was looted - Rs 20 lakh treasure, gold, jewels taken to Belagavi. Kittur fort was destroyed partially.

Jail Life: Rani was kept in Bailhongal Jail (40 km from Belagavi) - a small dark prison. She was kept in chains but still prayed daily for Kittur's freedom. She was not allowed to meet anyone. She died in same jail on 21 Feb 1829 due to illness and poor food at age 50. She spent 4 years in jail. Her adopted son Shivalingappa was also jailed and died later.

Sangolli Rayanna's Revenge: After Rani's arrest, her loyal commander Sangolli Rayanna continued guerrilla war for 5 years (1829-1830) with small army. He looted British treasury and distributed to poor. British finally captured him with help of his own relative and hanged him on 26 Jan 1831 near Nandgad - His last words "I die for my Rani and my land".

6. Weapons, Army & Why She Is Special

Weapons: Used 40 kg double-edged sword (Khadga), spear, and bow. She could fight with two swords on horseback - She practiced Kusti daily.
Army: 8000 soldiers including women soldiers - She trained women in Kittur - 500 women army called Veerangana Pade. Her generals were Amatur Balappa, Gurusiddappa, Sangolli Rayanna - all Lingayats and Marathas together.
Fort: Kittur Fort built in 1665 - Had 12 bastions, 3 moats, and secret tunnels - Still ruins exist near Belagavi.
Why First: She was first Indian ruler (man or woman) to fight and win against British East India Company in 1824 - Before 1857 revolt, before Lakshmibai (1858), before anyone. British records call her "Most determined rebel".
Memorials: Kittur Fort ruins, Chennamma Samadhi at Bailhongal (has Lingayat style tomb), Statue in Parliament House (2007), Statue at Kittur. Kittur Utsava is celebrated every year on 23 Oct (her birth and victory day). Karnataka government has Rani Chennamma University in Belagavi. Indian Navy ship "INAS Chennamma" named after her.
In Folk Songs: Lavani and Gejje songs in Karnataka still sing "Kittur Chennamma - Kittur's tigress". She is considered incarnation of Goddess Durga in North Karnataka.
She ruled just 8 years as queen but became immortal - At 46, she defeated British, at 50 died in jail but never surrendered.

"Kittur is not for sale, Kittur is my mother's land, I will not give it to firangis."
- Rani Chennamma, Letter to British Collector Thackeray, 1824
